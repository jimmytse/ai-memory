# Biblical Recipes for Healthy Living — Indonesia Ingredients

## Purpose
Develop truthful, nutritious, practical modern recipes inspired by foods documented in the Bible or strongly connected to biblical-era food traditions, adapted for ingredients readily available and preferably growable in Indonesia.

## Core principle
**Adapt the ingredient, but preserve the function.**
Preserve the original nutritional/practical role where reasonably possible; do not claim modern adaptations are literal biblical recipes.

## Truth & research standard
- Cite the exact biblical passage where applicable.
- Separate biblical fact, historical interpretation, scientific evidence, and modern adaptation.
- Avoid unsupported health/cure/medical claims.
- For important substitutions: original → Indonesian alternative → preserved function/benefit → limitations.
- Assess protein, fiber, healthy fats, carbohydrates, key vitamins/minerals, calories where practical, and nutritional gaps.
- Check Indonesian growability, climate/soil practicality, sustainability, affordability, and whether growing or buying is better.
- Check preparation and food safety: soaking, cooking, fermentation/sprouting, toxins/antinutrients, and storage.
- Before finalizing, actively check for better Indonesian ingredients or methods.
- Record exact quantities, servings, preparation, cooking, storage, safety, substitutions, changes/why, and future improvements.

## Developed recipes

### 1. Ezekiel-inspired whole-grain/legume bread
**Biblical reference:** Ezekiel 4:9.
The passage names wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and spelt (translation-dependent), but gives no complete modern bread recipe. Treat Ezekiel bread as a modern adaptation.

**Practical 1-loaf recipe (about 10–12 slices)**
- 200 g whole-wheat flour
- 75 g barley flour
- 50 g millet/jewawut flour
- 40 g cooked mung beans, very well drained and mashed
- 40 g cooked lentils, very well drained and mashed
- 7 g instant dry yeast
- 8 g salt
- 15 g honey (optional)
- 15 g cooking oil (optional; improves softness)
- 250–300 ml warm water, added gradually

**Preparation**
1. If starting from dry mung beans/lentils, rinse, soak separately, then cook until completely soft. Drain very well and cool before weighing. Do not put raw or undercooked beans into the dough.
2. Mix the whole-wheat, barley, and millet flours with yeast and salt.
3. Mash the cooled cooked legumes into a smooth paste; mix with honey, oil, and 250 ml warm water.
4. Add wet ingredients to dry ingredients. Mix until a sticky, soft dough forms. Add remaining water a little at a time; whole-grain flour absorbs water differently, so do not force all the water in.
5. Knead about 8–10 minutes by hand (or 5–7 minutes with a mixer) until cohesive and elastic. It will remain somewhat heavier/stickier than white-bread dough.
6. Put in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let rise in a warm place until roughly doubled, usually 60–90 minutes in a warm Indonesian kitchen.
7. Deflate gently, shape into one tight loaf, and place in a greased ~22 × 11 cm loaf pan.
8. Cover and proof again 30–60 minutes, until visibly puffy.
9. Bake at 180°C in a preheated oven for about 35–45 minutes. The loaf should be well browned and sound hollow when tapped; if using a thermometer, aim for about 93–96°C in the center.
10. Remove from the pan and cool completely on a rack before slicing. Cooling prevents a gummy center.
11. Store sealed at room temperature for about 1–2 days in a hot/humid climate; refrigerate for several days or freeze sliced portions for longer storage.

**Important adaptation notes**
- Ezekiel 4:9 is the biblical inspiration, not a complete recipe. The exact ancient proportions, milling, leavening, and baking method are unknown.
- The recipe intentionally keeps the biblical grain/legume concept while using Indonesian-friendly millet/jewawut and mung beans. Lentils and barley may be purchased.
- Cooked legumes are used rather than raw legumes for safety and texture. This changes the modern bread process but preserves the legume contribution.
- If barley flour is unavailable: replace it with whole-grain sorghum or additional whole-wheat flour. If millet is unavailable: use whole-grain sorghum or corn flour. These are modern functional substitutions, not biblical equivalences.
- For a more self-sufficient Indonesian version, test whole-grain sorghum/millet + mung bean + soybean/other locally grown legume, then compare texture and nutrition before replacing the closer version.

**Expected characteristics**
Dense, moist, nutty, high-fiber whole-grain bread rather than a light commercial loaf. Exact nutrition depends on flour composition, legume moisture, slice size, and optional ingredients.

**Food safety**
Use clean ingredients and fully cooked legumes. Do not use raw/undercooked kidney/red beans. Cool bread promptly before sealed storage; discard if mold develops.

### 2. Genesis 25:34 — Indonesian mung-bean lentil-style stew
**Biblical reference:** Genesis 25:34 — Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew; Esau ate and drank. The Bible does not give the recipe, quantities, spices, or cooking method.
**Modern adaptation:** mung beans + brown rice + Indonesian garden vegetables/aromatics.
**4 servings:** 300 g dried mung beans; ~400 g cooked brown rice; 150 g tomato; 100–150 g kangkung/spinach; 1 onion; 4 garlic cloves; 20 g ginger; 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tbsp oil; 1–1.2 L water; ½–1 tsp salt; optional pepper/coriander/lime/chili.
**Method:** rinse and optionally soak mung beans 4–8 h; cook in fresh water until completely soft; sauté aromatics/turmeric and tomato; combine with beans and water and simmer 10–15 min; add greens near the end; season and serve with ~100 g cooked brown rice per person.
**Approx. per serving:** ~320 kcal, ~16 g protein, ~16 g fiber, ~47 g carbohydrate before exact vegetables/oil; actual values vary.
**Strengths/gaps:** strong fiber, useful plant protein and whole-grain carbohydrate; provides folate, iron, magnesium and potassium. Not nutritionally complete alone; vary protein sources. Tempeh, egg, or fish can be optional additions, not biblical claims.
**Indonesia fit:** mung bean is a short ~55–65 day tropical crop and practical for home production; kangkung, tomato, ginger and turmeric are also practical garden crops. Brown rice is generally better purchased unless rice-growing infrastructure exists.
**Food safety:** cook legumes thoroughly, refrigerate promptly, and freeze for longer storage. Kidney/red beans require particular caution because raw/undercooked beans can contain high lectin levels.
**Sustainability:** legumes can support soil fertility through biological nitrogen fixation and fit useful crop rotations.
**Future refinement:** test a maximum-self-sufficiency version using home-grown mung bean + local staple + garden vegetables/aromatics, while keeping the biblical/adaptation distinction explicit.
