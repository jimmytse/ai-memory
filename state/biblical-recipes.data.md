## 1. Ezekiel-inspired whole-grain/legume bread
Source: Ezekiel 4:9 (names wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, spelt; translation-dependent; no complete recipe). Modern adaptation only. Accepted 2026-09-30.
Yield: 1 loaf, about 10–12 slices.
Ingredients: 200 g whole-wheat flour; 75 g barley flour; 50 g millet/jewawut flour; 40 g cooked mung beans (very well drained, mashed); 40 g cooked lentils (very well drained, mashed); 7 g instant dry yeast; 8 g salt; 15 g honey (optional); 15 g cooking oil (optional, improves softness); 250–300 ml warm water, added gradually.
Method: 1) If starting from dry mung beans/lentils: rinse, soak separately, cook until completely soft; drain very well and cool before weighing. Never put raw or undercooked beans in the dough. 2) Mix the three flours with yeast and salt. 3) Mash cooled legumes to a smooth paste; mix with honey, oil, and 250 ml warm water. 4) Add wet to dry; mix to a sticky, soft dough; add remaining water a little at a time (whole-grain flour absorbs unevenly). 5) Knead 8–10 min by hand (5–7 min mixer) until cohesive and elastic; it stays heavier and stickier than white-bread dough. 6) Rise covered in a warm place until roughly doubled, usually 60–90 min in a warm Indonesian kitchen. 7) Deflate gently, shape one tight loaf, place in a greased ~22 × 11 cm pan. 8) Proof covered 30–60 min until visibly puffy. 9) Bake at 180°C, preheated, 35–45 min; well browned and hollow-sounding when tapped (thermometer: about 93–96°C in the center). 10) Remove from pan and cool completely on a rack before slicing (prevents a gummy center).
Storage: sealed at room temperature 1–2 days in a hot/humid climate; refrigerate for several days; freeze sliced portions for longer.
Substitutions (modern functional, not biblical equivalences): no barley flour → whole-grain sorghum or more whole-wheat flour; no millet → whole-grain sorghum or corn flour.
Expected: dense, moist, nutty, high-fiber whole-grain bread, not a light commercial loaf. Nutrition depends on flour composition, legume moisture, slice size, optional ingredients.
Indonesia fit: millet/jewawut and mung beans are Indonesian-friendly; lentils and barley may be purchased.
Safety: use clean ingredients and fully cooked legumes; never raw/undercooked kidney/red beans; cool bread promptly before sealed storage; discard if mold develops.
Refine: test whole-grain sorghum/millet + mung bean + soybean or another local legume for a more self-sufficient version; compare texture and nutrition before replacing the closer version.

## 2. Genesis 25:34 — Indonesian mung-bean lentil-style stew
Source: Genesis 25:34 (Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew; Esau ate and drank). The Bible gives no recipe, quantities, spices, or method. Modern adaptation only. Accepted 2026-09-30.
Yield: 4 servings.
Ingredients: 300 g dried mung beans; ~400 g cooked brown rice; 150 g tomato; 100–150 g kangkung/spinach; 1 onion; 4 garlic cloves; 20 g ginger; 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tbsp oil; 1–1.2 L water; ½–1 tsp salt; optional pepper/coriander/lime/chili.
Method: rinse and optionally soak mung beans 4–8 h; cook in fresh water until completely soft; sauté aromatics/turmeric and tomato; combine with beans and water and simmer 10–15 min; add greens near the end; season; serve with ~100 g cooked brown rice per person.
Approx. per serving (before exact vegetables/oil; actual values vary): ~320 kcal, ~16 g protein, ~16 g fiber, ~47 g carbohydrate.
Strengths/gaps: strong fiber, useful plant protein and whole-grain carbohydrate; provides folate, iron, magnesium, potassium. Not nutritionally complete alone; vary protein sources. Tempeh, egg, or fish are optional additions, not biblical claims.
Indonesia fit: mung bean is a short (~55–65 day) tropical crop, practical for home production; kangkung, tomato, ginger, turmeric are practical garden crops; brown rice is generally better purchased unless rice-growing infrastructure exists.
Safety: cook legumes thoroughly; refrigerate promptly; freeze for longer storage. Kidney/red beans need particular caution (raw/undercooked can carry high lectin levels).
Sustainability: legumes support soil fertility through biological nitrogen fixation and fit useful crop rotations.
Refine: test a maximum-self-sufficiency version (home-grown mung bean + local staple + garden vegetables/aromatics), keeping the biblical/adaptation distinction explicit.
